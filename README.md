# EE22005 Engineering Practice and Design (Year 2)

Coursework portfolio from my second year of BEng Integrated Mechanical and Electrical Engineering at the University of Bath (2025-26). It collects evidence of technical and professional engineering skills across PCB design, signal integrity, additive manufacturing, hardware description languages, machine learning, programming and design process.

## Highlights

### PCB design, signal integrity and design for manufacture (Cadence OrCAD / Allegro)
Second-year PCB work in the Cadence OrCAD and Allegro tool chain: footprint and part symbol creation, schematic capture, layer stackup definition, board outline and routing setup, and a routed multilayer board.
- **Signal integrity changes to the layout:** decoupling capacitors placed close to the microcontroller power pins to reduce noise and keep the supply stable; more space between traces to reduce interference; no sharp right-angle traces; a ground plane added to give signals a better return path.
- **Design for manufacture:** enough component spacing for soldering and assembly, standard footprint sizes taken from datasheets, short clear traces, and a clear board outline and component orientation.
- **Justification for a multilayer board:** separate inner power and ground planes reduce noise and improve signal stability for amplifier circuits, and allow easier routing in less space. Low-cost multilayer fabrication (for example JLCPCB) keeps the cost reasonable.
- **Timer-based LED flasher (555) on USB-C power:** bill of materials (0805 resistors and capacitor, NE555 timer, LED, USB-C receptacle, four mounting holes), functional grouping, a decoupling capacitor next to the IC, short direct traces with few vias and a ground plane.
- **Assembly and test:** the populated board mixed surface-mount and through-hole parts. Some joints were clean and others showed minor unevenness and slight misalignment, which I noted as points to improve. Testing confirmed the board met its electrical requirements.

### Additive manufacturing (3D printing)
Prepared an STL model for FDM printing in PrusaSlicer.
- Oriented the part on its flattest, broadest face to reduce warping and improve bed adhesion, and to minimise overhangs.
- Supports set to "Everywhere" for unavoidable overhangs and bridges, 15% infill to balance material use, weight and strength, 100% scale, and no skirt or brim because the contact area was large enough.
- Checked the slicing preview (perimeters, infill pattern, support placement) before exporting. Result: about 1 h 3 min print time and about 3.7 m of filament.
- File: `3d_print_holder_newV1_nob_magnetic.gcode` (the exported G-code, generated by PrusaSlicer 2.9.0).

### Hardware description language (HDL) and FPGA
- Combinational logic written in an HDL and checked with a waveform simulation: output Z goes high when (A and B) is true or when C or D is high, which matched the specified logic.
- A button-controlled counter implemented on an FPGA board, with push-button input handling, clock control and 7-segment display output. Several modules (input processing, counter, display driver) were integrated into one interactive system that updates on each button press.

### Machine learning: breast tumour classification
Classification of malignant versus benign tumours on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin (Diagnostic) dataset (569 samples, 30 features) with a feedforward neural network.
- Trained with stochastic gradient descent with momentum and a cross-entropy loss, for 200 epochs on an 80/20 split.
- Hyperparameters chosen by grid search: 10 hidden units, learning rate 0.01, batch size 32.
- Reported results: 97.4% accuracy, precision 0.98, recall 0.96, F1-score 0.97, with 5 misclassifications out of 114 test samples.
- Discussion of improvements: dropout or L2 regularisation, feature selection or PCA, ensemble methods, and more diverse patient data.
- Files: `ml_code_screenshot_1.png`, `ml_code_screenshot_2.png`
